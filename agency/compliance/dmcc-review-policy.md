This policy operationalises the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024
("DMCC Act") banned-practices regime for consumer reviews (Schedule 20, in force from
6 April 2025) for everyone who deals with customer reviews at [Client Business Name].
The Competition and Markets Authority ("CMA") enforces this regime and can impose fines
of up to 10% of global turnover for breaches.

---

## 1. What the law requires (plain English)

Under the DMCC Act it is a banned practice to:

1. Submit or commission a **fake review** — one that does not reflect a genuine
   experience.
2. Submit or commission a review that **conceals an incentive** was given for it.
3. **Publish reviews in a misleading way** — including suppressing genuine reviews or
   selectively promoting only positive (or only negative) ones.
4. **Fail to take reasonable and proportionate steps** to prevent and remove fake or
   hidden-incentive reviews, if you publish or give access to reviews at all.
5. **Offer a service to another trader** to carry out or facilitate any of the above.

(Statutory reference: DMCC Act 2024, Schedule 20, paragraph 13; commencement confirmed
6 April 2025 by S.I. 2025/272. See `agency/compliance/legal-research-notes.md` §1 for
full sourcing.)

## 2. [Client Business Name]'s rules — no exceptions

### 2.1 No fake or incentivised reviews (DMCC-1)

- We never pay, discount, or reward a customer — in any form, disclosed or not — for
  leaving a review.
- We never submit a review ourselves, or ask a friend, family member, supplier, or
  employee to post a review purporting to be an independent customer experience.
- We never enter customers into a prize draw, offer a free add-on, or offer any other
  benefit conditional (in whole or in part) on leaving a review.

### 2.2 No review gating (DMCC-2)

- Every customer we invite to review is invited to leave a **public** review, via the
  same link, regardless of how happy or unhappy they seemed.
- We never ask "How was your experience?" first and only send the public review link to
  customers who answer positively.
- We never route an unhappy customer to a private feedback form **instead of** the public
  review ask. A private feedback channel may exist **in addition to** the public ask,
  never as a replacement, and never sequenced so a negative internal answer suppresses
  the public invitation.
- We never selectively promote, hide, or bury genuine reviews based on their star rating.

### 2.3 No paying to alter or remove reviews (DMCC-3)

- We never offer a refund, payment, discount, or other benefit conditional on a customer
  editing or deleting a review.
- Making things right for a genuinely unhappy customer (an apology, a fix, a refund for
  the underlying problem) is good service and is always fine — it must never be framed
  or timed as a condition of the review being changed or removed.

### 2.4 Reasonable and proportionate steps (DMCC-4)

As a business that publishes/gives access to reviews, we take the following steps,
proportionate to our size:

- We only invite customers we can verify actually transacted with us (see
  `agency/ghl/workflows/review-request-sequence.md` eligibility check).
- We keep a send log of every review-request message (recipient, date, transaction
  reference) so we can demonstrate who was genuinely invited and when.
- We periodically check our published reviews for anything that looks suspicious (e.g. a
  review referencing a transaction we have no record of) and report it to the review
  platform (Google) for investigation.
- We review the effectiveness of these steps at least [annually / quarterly — Client to
  set] and update this policy if needed.
- We do not treat limited resources as an excuse for having no process at all — CMA
  guidance is explicit that businesses "cannot justify inaction based on a lack of
  resources."

### 2.5 No impersonating customers (DMCC-5)

- We never draft, ghost-write, or supply pre-written text for a customer's review.
- We never post a review on a customer's behalf, from any account, including
  agency-controlled or staff-controlled accounts.
- Genuine customer testimonials used in marketing must be attributed accurately and
  with the customer's permission — never presented as an independent, unprompted review
  if it was solicited by us.

## 3. Staff do's and don'ts

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Invite every eligible customer to review, using the standard message sequence | Ask only customers you think will leave a good review |
| Say "we'd love your honest feedback" | Say or imply "please leave us 5 stars" |
| Log every review request sent | Offer anything — a discount, a gift, a free service — for a review |
| Escalate a negative review to a manager for a genuine resolution | Offer to fix things *in exchange for* changing/removing the review |
| Report a suspicious review (one you don't recognise) to the platform | Post, edit, or ask someone else to post a review pretending to be a customer |
| Keep the public review ask identical for every customer | Send a different, softer or harder ask depending on how the job went |

## 4. Platform terms (supporting note — TONE-2)

Google's own review policy independently prohibits incentivising reviews and prohibits
"discouraging or prohibiting negative reviews, or selectively soliciting positive
reviews" (i.e. gating). Breaching this policy risks review removal or Business Profile
suspension by Google, **in addition to** DMCC Act liability. (See
`agency/compliance/legal-research-notes.md` §5 for sourcing.)
