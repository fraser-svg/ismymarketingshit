# Review Response Templates

Ready-to-use templates for replying to customer reviews on Google Business Profile. The client's staff or the agency (as the client's disclosed agent) will post these responses in the client's name.

**Critical:** Responses must be authored and posted by the business (or the agency on the business's explicit instruction as a disclosed representative) — never impersonate a customer or fake a customer voice. The response identifies the business, not the reviewer.

---

## Positive Review Responses

Use these when a customer leaves a positive review (4–5 stars). Personalise with the reviewer's first name (if shown) and any specific detail they mentioned.

---

### Positive Response Template 1 — Grateful & personal

```
Thank you so much, [Name/you]! We really appreciate you taking the time to share your feedback. It's wonderful to hear that we got it right for you — that kind of honest feedback is what keeps us going. We'd love to see you again soon!
```

---

### Positive Response Template 2 — Specific detail acknowledged

```
Thanks, [Name/you], for the kind words about [specific thing they mentioned — service, staff, quality, etc.]. Hearing that we nailed that particular aspect means a lot to our team. We're grateful for your business and hope to see you again.
```

---

### Positive Response Template 3 — Warm & inviting

```
[Name/you], thank you for the lovely review! We really do try hard to get it right every time, and feedback like yours reminds us why we do what we do. We'd genuinely love to see you again — please come back and say hi.
```

### Positive Response Template 4 — Professional & brief

```
Thanks for taking the time to review us, [Name/you]. We're delighted you had a positive experience. Your feedback is really valued, and we look forward to working with you again.
```

### Positive Response Template 5 — Team celebration

```

```

## Negative Review Responses

Use these when a customer leaves a negative review (1–3 stars). The goal is to:
1. **Empathise** — show you understand they're disappointed.
2. **Own it** — don't make excuses; acknowledge what went wrong.
3. **Take it offline** — invite direct contact to resolve it properly.
4. **Never** offer payment, refund, or benefit conditional on them editing or removing the review.

Personalise with the reviewer's first name (if shown) and any specific detail they mentioned.

---

### Negative Response Template 1 — Empathetic & solution-focused

```
[Name/you], we're genuinely sorry to hear this. Your feedback is completely fair, and we take it seriously. Rather than respond here, we'd really like to make this right with you properly. Could you please get in touch directly at [contact email/phone]? We'd like the chance to understand what happened and put things right.
```

---

### Negative Response Template 2 — Acknowledge & escalate

```
Thanks for taking the time to leave this feedback, [Name/you]. We're disappointed to hear we let you down. We'd like to understand what went wrong and have a proper conversation about it. Please reach out to us at [contact email/phone] — our team will prioritise your message.
```

---

### Negative Response Template 3 — Apologetic & direct

```
[Name/you], thank you for being honest. We're sorry we didn't meet your expectations this time. This isn't the experience we want anyone to have, and we'd like to talk through what happened and how we can do better. Please get in touch at [contact email/phone] — we're listening.
```

---

### Negative Response Template 4 — Accountability & next steps

```
We appreciate this feedback, [Name/you]. We're clearly not happy with this outcome either, and we take your concerns seriously. Rather than reply here, we'd like to speak with you directly to understand exactly what went wrong and what we can do. Could you contact us at [contact email/phone]? We're committed to putting this right.
```

---

### Negative Response Template 5 — Humble & invitation

```
[Name/you], thank you for telling us. You're right to expect better, and we're sorry we didn't deliver it. We genuinely do care about getting things right, and we've clearly missed the mark this time. We'd welcome the chance to speak with you directly about this. Please reach out at [contact email/phone].
```

## Usage guidance

### When to use these templates

- **Positive responses** should go on most 4–5 star reviews, particularly those that mention specific staff members, service aspects, or business strengths.
- **Negative responses** should go on all 1–3 star reviews. Responding to negatives is important: it shows you take feedback seriously and gives you a chance to resolve issues away from public view.

### Before posting

1. **Personalise it.** If the reviewer used their name, use it. If they mentioned something specific (a staff member's name, a service detail, a problem), reference it in your response.
2. **Keep it brief.** Responses under 300 characters read better on mobile and feel less corporate.
3. **Match the tone.** Keep it warm and conversational — imagine you're texting a customer, not writing a corporate memo.
4. **Proof-read.** Check for spelling and grammar before posting; responses reflect the business.

### For negative reviews — do this, not that

**Do this:**
- Acknowledge what they said and apologise for the experience.
- Take responsibility ("We didn't get this right").
- Invite direct contact to resolve it.
- Follow up when they contact you — track the complaint and work toward a genuine fix.

**Do NOT do this:**
- Deny their experience or argue with them.
- Offer a discount, refund, or benefit conditional on them removing or editing the review.
- Pretend the problem didn't happen or was their fault.
- Respond defensively or dismissively.
- Ignore complaints that come via direct contact after they've left a review.

### Legal note (DMCC-3 & DMCC-5)

- **No payment for review removal:** The agency and client must never offer refunds, discounts, free services, or other benefits in exchange for a customer editing or removing a negative review. Doing so breaches UK consumer law (DMCC-3). Offering to fix a genuine problem is fine; offering to pay for the review's removal is not.
- **Agency acts as disclosed agent:** If the agency is posting responses on the client's behalf, this must be documented in the client's instructions and visible in the account setup. Responses are always posted as from the business, not from an agency representative acting independently.

### Implementation in GHL

1. Copy the relevant template(s) to a shared document or internal notes within the GHL contact record.
2. When a new review arrives (via GHL's Reputation Management module or manual notification), the assigned staff member customises the template and posts it via the GHL interface or Google Business Profile directly.
3. Document the response in the contact's notes (GHL task, note, or log) so you have a record of which reviews received responses and when.
4. Track negative-review follow-ups: if a customer responds to your message or contacts you directly after a negative review, log this and prioritise resolution within 48 hours.

## Compliance notes

- **DMCC-3:** No response template suggests, implies, or offers payment, refund, discount, or benefit conditional on editing or removing a review.
- **DMCC-5:** Responses are authored by the business (or the agency as a disclosed representative) and posted in the business's name — never pretending to be a customer or using a fake customer account.
